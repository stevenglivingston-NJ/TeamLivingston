What the data says

Six findings, in the order they cost you money

1

These do not look like customer cancellations. They look like a schedule being cleared.

Real customer cancellations arrive one at a time, at random hours, scattered across the week. These don't. 39 of 86 landed in a tight cluster with at least one other cancellation — five wiped out in six minutes on Fri Jun 12, four in fifteen minutes on Wed Aug 5. They cluster Tuesday–Friday and essentially never happen on Monday (4 of 86). The median lands 20.5 hours before the appointment: late afternoon, the day before.

Cancellation weekday · Thu 23 · Fri 20 · Wed 20 · Tue 18 · Mon 4 · Sat 1 · Sun 0 Cancellation hour · concentrated 12:00–17:00 and 19:00–20:00
2

The reason field is empty on 69 of 86, and ServiceMinder has no cancel-reason report to fall back on.

Only 8 cancellations have an actual customer-stated reason anywhere in the system — all 8 came in through Perceptionist call summaries, not from anyone in the office logging it. Another 9 carry a bare "Other" or a data-cleanup note. The picklist was used 25 times out of 126, and 24 of those were "Other". You cannot manage a 58% cancellation rate you have no reasons for.

Customer-stated reason found · 8 | "Other" / admin note only · 9 | Nothing at all · 69
3

The cancel rate tracks the zip code. That is the cherry-picking signal.

In the affluent Essex core the rep attends roughly six of ten booked consults. In the urban and Hudson-county towns he attends almost none. Glen Ridge and West Caldwell run 17% cancelled; East Orange 80%, Hillside 100%, Union 100%, Jersey City 100%, Secaucus 100%. Volume is small in each individual town, but the gradient is consistent across the whole outer ring and it runs the same direction every time.

Inner affluent ring (Glen Ridge, W. Caldwell, W. Orange, Livingston, Verona) · 28 booked · 32% cancelled Urban / Hudson (E. Orange, Newark, Hillside, Union, Jersey City, Bayonne, N. Bergen, Secaucus) · 16 booked · 88% cancelled
4

There is one written admission of disqualifying a lead, and it is still shaping decisions five years later.

Yvette Bailey's contact record carries a 2021 note titled "Cancel Appt": "Prospect can get cabinets for $5200 plus installation… not possible. Did not waste any more time with this lady. Wacky." Her 2026 record then reads: "I'm hesitant to reach back out bc the notes from 2021 states waste of time and 'wacky'." She rebooked in August 2026 with a $30–50k stated budget. That appointment was cancelled three hours before the slot.

5

A third of the export is test data, and it is hiding in your cancellation rate.

40 of 126 rows are integration tests — "test fallback 1" with phone 22222222222222, "test integration", "Testing123 Please1 - KTU", "Admin Javalogix Test". They were created through the KTUApp API key and by Support, all booked to the Bloomfield office address, all cancelled. Anyone pulling a cancellation report from ServiceMinder without stripping these is reading a number that is 32% fiction.

6

Booking integrity is broken upstream — wrong addresses, double-books, appointments that were never written at all.

Four August leads (Camille Howard, Tanvi Thacker, Sara & Mike Corrigan, Sher Manara) had the KTU office address written onto their contact record instead of their home; the real addresses had to be recovered from HighLevel by hand. Marilyn Vargas and Yvette Bailey were both booked into Wed Aug 26 at 10:00 AM. Kerri Palen was told by Perceptionist she had a 2 PM Friday consult that was never written to ServiceMinder at all — nobody showed up. Jackie Giordano's own call summary went to a duplicate record in the BTU organization.

The cost

What 128 cancelled consults are worth

Of the 90 prospects who actually got an in-home consult, 18 are customers today — a 20% close rate off the appointment. Of the 75 who were cancelled, exactly one is a customer, and that one had already completed three earlier consults. Sixty-nine were never rebooked and never contacted again.

On attribution: ServiceMinder's export and its API expose when an appointment was cancelled but not who cancelled it — there is no user stamp on the cancel action. Every conclusion above about who is doing the cancelling is behavioral inference from timing, batching and geography, not a name in a log. Pulling the ServiceMinder UI activity log for the fifteen batch windows listed above would settle it in an afternoon, and that is the first thing to do before any conversation with the rep.

What to do

Next steps, in order

Pull the ServiceMinder activity log for the 15 batch windows

The batch timestamps are listed above to the minute. The UI audit trail names the user. This turns inference into fact before anyone is accused of anything.

Make Cancel Reason mandatory, and delete "Other"

The picklist was used 25 times in 126 appointments and 24 of those were "Other". Build a real list — customer postponed, customer unreachable, out of area, budget mismatch, duplicate, rep reschedule — and require it on cancel.

Take cancel rights away from the sales seat

Route every cancellation through the office. A rep who wants out of an appointment files a request with a reason; someone else executes it. This alone would have produced 69 reasons you currently don't have.

Work the 69 dead leads this week

They are sitting in ServiceMinder as Prospects with nothing scheduled and nothing logged. At the attended close rate they are worth roughly 14 customers. Start with the 40 that were killed the day before — those people still expected a visit.

Purge the 40 test bookings and lock down the KTUApp key

They corrupt every cancellation report anyone runs. Delete them, and stop integration tests writing live appointments to the Bloomfield office address.

Fix the geo-targeting and the address write-back

Eight of twelve out-of-area bookings came from paid channels. And four August leads had the office address written to their contact record instead of their home — that is a HighLevel-to-ServiceMinder mapping bug, not a rep problem.
