ServiceMinder JobTread Automated Pre-Selection · ends when the client gets their pick-lists

How a bathroom job travels from the first appointment to the moment the client opens their selections. Everything after that — their picks, change orders, ordering, install and the final invoice — is covered by the Post-Selection process.

Who owns what

ServiceMinder Everything the client pays

Appointments, the proposal and signed contract, invoices, payments, change orders. If it is a dollar the client owes, it lives here.

JobTread Everything the client chooses

Job parameters, the production budget, selection documents, jobsite photos, schedule. If it is a decision about what gets installed, it lives here.

The sync Turns funding into choices

Reads the parameters and the budget, then writes the client's pick-lists with photos and delta pricing. Runs itself every 30 minutes.

The eight steps

Each step names the system, the owner, and the one condition that has to hold before the job moves forward.

01

Lead intake and screening

ServiceMinderOffice

Book the consultation — In-Home (2 hrs), Virtual, In-Store or Showroom. Before anyone quotes, screen the job against the acceptance rules.

  • Bathroom stays in the same footprint — no walls moving
  • Plumbing fixtures stay in their exact locations
  • Electrical is fixture swap only — no new circuits
  • No fan additions, radiator moves or other HVAC changes
  • No exterior work, and the client expects a 2–3 week window
StopAny one of these failing makes it a structural job, not a Bath Tune-Up job. Escalate to Steven before quoting — never design around it.
02

Sales appointment

ServiceMinderSales / designer

On site: measure, confirm the footprint and fixture locations, and set the tier for each category — Essential, Enhanced or Elite. That tier decision is the most important output of this visit. It sets the price now and decides exactly which products the client can choose from later.

  • Establish what is in scope: tub, shower, vanity, toilet, floor, mirror or cabinet, vent fan
  • Electrical goes to Robert, billed direct to the client
  • Plumbing beyond an in-place swap goes to Craig, billed direct
  • Book a Measuring appointment if the vanity or wall system needs one
Move on whenScope fits the standards and every open category has a tier.
03

Design and internal sign-off

ServiceMinderKaren · design team

The design is a configuration inside the standard framework, not a bespoke build. Products come from the approved lines: Bertch, Aline or Hardware Resources vanities; ClassicRock or the vanity vendor for countertops; tile chosen at TileShop within the tier allowance; fixtures from the catalog.

No design fee is charged. ServiceMinder carries a $500 Design Service, but it is not billed on Bath Tune-Up jobs — design is part of the sale.

Move on whenThe team has signed off. The client never sees a design the team has not aligned on first.
04

Proposal built from the parts library

ServiceMinderSales / ops

Choose the service — Full Bathroom Remodel, Shower Remodel, Bathtub Remodel, Tub/Shower Conversion or Vanity Only Sales — then build the lines: demo, drywall, paint, install labor, contingency project fee, shipping and handling.

The allowance lines are the ones that matter downstream. A line reading “Allowance for tile from our Enhanced Series line items. $20 per sq. ft.” is what later funds the client's pick-list and decides what reads “included” versus “+$300”.

Move on whenEvery category the design opens has a funded line. A category with no money behind it silently disappears from the client's selections later.
05

Client signs

ServiceMinderSales

The client accepts the proposal and it becomes the contract. That accepted proposal is the reference point for every later conversation about scope — anything added after this is a change order.

Move on whenProposal accepted, scope locked.
06

Official invoice and deposit

ServiceMinderOps

With scope locked, raise the invoice from the accepted proposal — ServiceMinder keeps the proposal ID on the invoice so the two stay tied together. The client gets a pay link and a PDF; terms are due on receipt, and a card surcharge posts as its own line when they pay that way.

Payment is staged across the job in three parts. Credits for removed scope go against the final balance rather than being refunded mid-job.

50%on signing
40%start of install
10%on completion
Move on whenDeposit received. Nothing gets ordered before the money lands.
07

Build the job in JobTread

JobTreadSales rep

This is the handoff drawn at the top of the page, and the step where mistakes cost the most. The sales rep who sold the job builds it — they know what was promised, so they are the one who can catch a tier or an allowance that does not match the conversation. Two things get entered and they do different jobs:

  • Parameters — the tier for each category. These decide which products the client is offered.
  • Budget lines — the sold allowances. These decide how much is funded, so each option can be priced as “included” or “+$X”.
Move on whenBoth are saved and correct — then leave the job alone. The sync deliberately waits 30 minutes after the last budget edit so it never fires on a half-typed budget.
08

Selections generate and the client is invited

Automated Chief of Staff · sales rep

The sync audits the catalogue, then writes selection documents to every job that is ready — one pick-list per client decision, plus the optional upgrade lists, each with photos, plain-English descriptions and delta pricing.

Two human jobs sit on top of it. The Chief of Staff reads the run report daily — it names every job the sync skipped and why, and it is only worth producing if somebody acts on it. Then the sales rep or designer tells the client their selections are ready; the documents appear on their own, the invitation does not.

Hands off toThe Post-Selection process — the client makes their picks from here.

What “ready” means

The sync writes to a job only when all four of these hold. Any one missing and it leaves the job alone and says so in the run report.

1Not yet confirmed

Still draft or open. Once the client signs a selection, the job freezes.

2Something in scope

At least one category is open. Nothing to create is not a reason to write.

3Zero blocking flags

No under-funded category, no ADA hold, no empty pick-list, no invalidated pick.

4Budget has settled

No budget edits for 30 minutes, so a half-entered budget never goes out.

When the sync refuses to write

All deliberate. The system would rather leave a job alone than put a wrong number in front of a client. Every one of these is named in the run report.

Blocks
A category is under-funded by more than $200 The cheapest option costs more than the budget line covers, so the client would open their selections to a surprise upcharge. Fix the budget line — or decide to absorb it — before it goes out.
Blocks
An ADA kit is on the job Accessibility work never goes out automatically. It gets a human review first, every time.
Blocks
A parameter opens a category with nothing behind it Either the tier resolves to zero products or no budget line funds it. An empty pick-list is worse than none, so the job waits.
Blocks
The client's existing pick is no longer valid If scope changed underneath a choice they already made, the job stops rather than quietly swapping their selection.
Waits
The budget was edited in the last 30 minutes A budget mid-entry looks like a half-scoped job. The sync waits for it to settle, then proceeds on its own.
Reports only
Headroom, scope drops and duplicate SKUs Useful to know, not reasons to stop. They appear in the run report and the job still goes out.

📈 P&L — what the books actually say, month by month

Pulled straight from QuickBooks on a cash basis, per entity. Moola reads a P&L every morning to form its verdicts and then throws the statement away, so until now the judgements were visible and the numbers behind them were not. The column that decides how much of this to believe is unclassified — income and expense sitting in Uncategorized or Unapplied accounts, which is money the books have received but not yet explained. A month with a big unclassified figure has a net income you should not quote.
Loading…

🔍 Validated P&L — every unclassified dollar, named

The raw P&L above is what QuickBooks says. This is what the books mean once the Uncategorized and Unapplied accounts are traced back to the transactions behind them. Each line names the counterparty, the predicted classification and how confident that prediction is, so you can accept, correct or ignore it — nothing here has been changed in QuickBooks.
Loading…

📊 Common-size P&L — every line as a % of cash revenue

Percentages against validated cash revenue, so the denominator is money actually earned rather than money that merely arrived. The band is a residential-remodeling reference range — my figures, not HFC's: treat them as a starting point and overwrite them the moment you have the franchise's own. Heavy means above the band, lean below. Neither is automatically bad: lean marketing and lean royalties are very different problems.
Loading…

💵 Cash Flow — where cash has been and where it's going

Week-by-week running balance: today's actual cash, then every expected movement out to the horizon. Known = a dated bill or a signed tranche. Projected/estimated = pattern-based, so treat the far weeks as a shape, not a number. The low point is the number that matters — a trough three weeks out is still a problem you can solve today.

👑 Royalties — what we owe, what HFC claims, what actually left

Three independent numbers per month, because reconciling HFC's workbook to the bank debit only checks HFC against HFC — if their revenue basis is wrong, invoice and debit agree and the error is invisible. Ours is computed from our own ServiceMinder invoices. Fill HFC by parsing the monthly workbook, and Bank from the reconciliation.
